Overview

Project Icarus is a quadrotor I built from individually sourced parts. Alongside it I wrote a 6‑DoF quadrotor simulation in Python. I'm now porting the controller to C++.

Approach

Simulation

The Python simulation models the quadrotor in six degrees of freedom. It runs cascaded PID control and a complementary filter for attitude. A linear Kalman filter estimates position and velocity from noisy GPS.

LQR benchmark

To benchmark the PID loops I added a full-state LQR controller, linearized about hover. The control law is:

u = utrim − K (x − xref)

The input u is the wrench [T, τx, τy, τz]. K is a 4 × 12 gain matrix. The 12 states, in order, are [x, y, z, vx, vy, vz, φ, θ, ψ, p, q, r]. A mixer turns the wrench into motor commands.

C++ port

The C++ plant is written with Eigen and represents attitude as a quaternion. Three tests verify it: free fall, hover, and a 90° yaw turn. All three pass. The LQR gain is exported from the Python sim to a CSV, and the C++ controller loads it.

Hardware

I sourced the airframe parts individually: 2212 980KV motors, BLHeli_S ESCs and a CUAV V5 Nano flight controller. MAVProxy confirmed a heartbeat over UART at 921600 baud. A props-off arm test passed with all four motors spinning in the correct direction. The quad is registered with the FAA.

Results

~32%Faster mission time, LQR vs. PID, in sim

12States in the LQR model

3 / 3C++ plant verification tests passing

In simulation, LQR completed the waypoint mission about 32% faster than PID.

What didn't work

The PID and LQR runs used different noise seeds. Until I rerun both on the same seed, the 32% is indicative, not final.

RK4 integration isn't in the C++ plant yet.
